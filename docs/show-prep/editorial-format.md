# Episode openings and planned Shorts

Vincent's editorial direction, 2026-10-08: open with a clear viewer promise and a
useful visual, prepare clearer standalone Shorts topics, and bring at least one
controversial angle into every episode. Give the conversation boundaries that
make the recording easier to edit while keeping the hosts natural.

This applies to new episode preparation alongside the source-board contract in
[`prep`](../../skills/prep/SKILL.md). It is a show about how two founders use AI in
their businesses. Choose a central founder question; model news matters when it
changes that decision. Keep the hosts' humor, actual experience and disagreement.

## Open with the payoff

Use [Riley Brown's opening advice](https://x.com/rileybrown/status/2108223689783189848)
as an editorial hypothesis, not proof of improved retention. The hosts and a
relevant screenshot, source passage or ready result should be visible together.

- First 15 seconds: name the question and why the viewer cares.
- Next 20 seconds: give the reason to trust the discussion and the specific payoff.
  Use actual experience or evidence; promise only a ready demonstration.
- By about 45 seconds: show the three questions guiding the episode and begin
  the first useful point. These are timing cues, not a mandatory speech.
- Bring in greetings, chat and housekeeping after the first useful takeaway.

If a countdown or setup check is needed live, put it before the editorial opening
and mark it for removal from the replay. A short opening may be rehearsed; the
body uses source links and conversational cues rather than performed lines.

## Name the Shorts before recording

Prepare 3–5 narrow candidate angles per episode and map each to an existing
segment. These are planned opportunities, not a promise to publish that many
clips. Broad buckets such as “AI news” or “agent workflows” are not Shorts topics.
A viewer should understand the named subject and tension without the episode.

For each candidate, fill this card using `###` inside an app source section:

```markdown
### Planned Short S1 — Specific question or claim

- Working title: named subject plus one clear tension.
- Point: one question to answer or provisional claim to challenge.
- Open: the source passage or actual experience to show; state what it establishes.
- Challenge: the strongest competing interpretation; invite the co-host's real view.
- Payoff: the decision or conditional verdict to reach; leave the answer open until discussed.
- Capture: parent segment; self-contained entry; intended exit after the answer and its caveat.
- After recording: coverage and exact start/end unverified; verify against the video/VTT.
```

Aim for roughly 45–90 seconds for the finished Short when the point fits. This is
an editing target, not a platform limit or a reason to cut essential context.
Let the source discussion run longer when needed; capture a concise answer before
moving to the next question. Do not add a second timed segment for its clip card.

## Give every episode something worth arguing about

Choose at least one debatable claim with a consequence for founders: wasted
spend, slower delivery, misplaced trust, dependence on a vendor, or a workflow
habit worth dropping. State the strongest opposing case and what would change
the conclusion. If evidence is missing, frame it as a question or hypothesis and
make the missing evidence explicit.

Plan the controversial question, not the hosts' disagreement. Neither host needs
to defend a view they do not hold. The final verdict can reject the proposed take.
Keep the claim as strong as the evidence allows; a provocative title must still
describe what the recording supports.

Illustrative angles for an agent-workflow episode, to investigate rather than
announce as results:

| Working Short title | Question / tension | Evidence to bring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are More AI Agents Making You Slower? | Extra workers versus coordination and checking work | One actual task, its handoffs and retained result; timings only if recorded |
| Cheaper AI Tokens Aren't the Whole Bill | Unit price versus the effort to finish the task | Actual usage and rework, or a labelled hypothetical with no savings claim |
| When Should You Stop an AI Agent? | Persistence versus a loop that needs intervention | A real failed attempt, the stop decision and what happened next |
| When Is an AI Agent Actually Done? | A confident completion message versus a usable outcome | The delivered artifact and the check that accepts or rejects it |

## Record clean boundaries and hand them to the editor

- At the entry, name the subject again instead of relying on “that thing we said.”
- Finish one claim, example, challenge and takeaway before adding another topic.
  Keep qualifications beside the claim they qualify.
- Give the editor a short pause before changing topic or bringing chat back in.
  Keep tangents in the parking lot until the point has landed.
- Log rough actual markers when practical. Never turn planned elapsed ranges into
  recorded timestamps or infer that a displayed cue aired.
- After recording, give each viable candidate its actual start/end, supported
  title, proof shot, essential caveat and status: captured, needs context, or drop.
  Use [`clips`](../../skills/clips/SKILL.md) to verify and package the actual material.

For the replay, trim the countdown, setup and opening dead air; retain failures
and disagreement that explain the decision. Each standalone cut needs its own
context and payoff. Do not stitch separate answers into a stronger claim or
remove the qualification that makes the take accurate.

Track time spent finding clips, candidates that yielded complete cuts, and
first-minute retention across comparable episodes when analytics are available.
These are ways to assess the approach, not claimed results.
