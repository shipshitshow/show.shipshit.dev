## Cold Open — Facts card

- **Accenture:** $29 of Claude per employee per month, centrally funded, from 1 Oct. Above that, a client project or team pays. Some staff were spending up to $1,000 a month (404 Media, 15 Oct).
- **Same day:** Accenture beat earnings, said its AI and data workforce is about 110,000 people, and the stock jumped (State of AI, citing CNBC).
- **Why now:** Claude Enterprise seats now "cover access only, and all usage is billed separately at standard API rates" (Claude help page). Flat seats hid heavy users; metering puts each one on an invoice.
- **Others:** Uber spent its 2026 AI coding budget by April and caps $1,500 per person per tool (TNW, Jun). Every Microsoft division has a token cap since July and the default internal model moved to a cheaper one (TNW, Aug). Meta reportedly halved Claude Code users from 60,000 to 30,000 (TechRadar citing The Information).
- **The other side:** Anthropic merged about 2,500 changes a day in May and over 30,000 in September, mostly agent-written, and had to replace git to keep up (Anthropic engineering, 13 Oct).
- **Prices:** per-token prices are down about 98% since late 2022, yet enterprise AI bills have tripled (TNW, Aug). Gemini 4 Argon is $2/$10 per 1M tokens until 30 Nov, then $4/$20 (Google).

## Segment 3 — What 12x output looks like

### Question and opening cue

Anthropic is the opposite case: it is not capping, it is drowning in output. What happens when agents write most of the code?

### The numbers

- **[Anthropic engineering, 13 Oct](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control):** merges went from about 2,500 a day in May to over 30,000 in September. Main-branch history almost 100 GB; about 25 million CI checkouts a day. Seven people built a replacement for git, called Scion, in five months, and a review tool called Antique. Not open-sourced and "by no means a suitable replacement for git for general use".
- Claude wrote most of the code, including a rewrite of the ingestion pipeline at 20x less memory and 4x faster.
- The line to put on screen: "Very often, we found that the role of the engineers was to tell Claude 'no.'"

### Conversation cues

- Host challenge: is 30,000 merges a day productivity, or a firehose that needs a new review system to stay safe? [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/devops/2026/10/13/anthropic-reinvents-source-control-as-claude-floods-the-world-with-code/5302943) framed it as a problem Claude created and then fixed.
- Founder angle: you are not Anthropic, but the bottleneck move is the same. When AI writes faster, the cost moves to review, testing and deciding what not to build. Where did it move in your business?
- Budget: about 10 minutes. Exit cue: "if spend is going up anyway, can you at least pay less per job?"

## Segment 4 — Paying less per job

### Question and opening cue

Tokens are 98% cheaper than in 2022 and bills still tripled. Can routing to cheaper models fix the budget, or is it a trap?

### The numbers

- **[Gemini 4 Argon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon), Google, 13 Oct:** $2 per 1M input and $10 per 1M output until 30 Nov, then $4/$20. Cached input 95% off. API and AI Studio worldwide; Gemini app and AI Mode for Pro and Ultra. Google claims #1 on the Vals Index (finance, coding, legal, tax). Last week's Artificial Analysis note said Argon reads 2 to 7 times fewer input tokens per task than Muse Wonder.
- **Microsoft** moved its internal default to a cheaper OpenAI model (TNW, Aug).
- **OpenAI deprecations, 14 Oct:** GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1 mini leave the API on 14 Jan 2027, fine-tunes included; GPT-6 Luna is the suggested replacement ([digitalapplied summary](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gpt-4o-gpt-4-1-api-shutdown-january-2027); check OpenAI's deprecations page on air).

### Conversation cues

- Controversial claim: **intro pricing is a customer acquisition cost, not a price.** Argon doubles on 1 Dec. Build your budget on the standard rate. Strongest opposing case: switching is cheap now, and prices have only fallen over two years.
- The other trap: the cheap model you built on gets retired. Anyone with a product on GPT-4o has 12 weeks.
- Each host: do you route jobs to different models to save money today, or pay for one and stop thinking about it?
- Budget: about 10 minutes. Exit cue: "and why prices might not keep falling."

## Segment 5 — Who pays for the labs

### Question and opening cue

The labs need this spend to grow. What does that mean for your prices next year?

### Facts (reported, not filed)

- **Anthropic** (Reuters via [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/reuters-anthropic-ipo-investors-push-valuation-down-to-15t-from-2t-over-q3), 15 Oct): Q3 revenue $16.8B, up 45% on Q2's $11.6B. Investors are discussing about $1.5T for the November listing, down from about $2T. More than $8B operating loss last year and over $518B of cloud and compute committed over a decade.
- Enterprise customers capping use is exactly the slowdown investors are pricing in. The pressure runs both ways: labs want usage up, buyers want bills down.

### Conversation cues

- Hypothesis, not a finding: with that much compute committed, labs will push metered pricing harder, not cut it. What would prove that wrong?
- Keep it short; this is context for the budget, not an IPO segment.
- Budget: about 6 minutes. Exit cue: "so what number would we actually give a hire?"
