# Opening and hooks

The first sentence on air is the one line the hosts prepare and approve. It goes on the board under `## Cold Open`, next to the viewer question. Everything after it stays conversational.

## The rule

1. **Countdown or holding card first, if the stream has one.** It is deliberate. It is not dead air and it is not the opening.
2. **First spoken sentence = the promise, said to the lens.** A claim plus the stake for the viewer. Under 15 seconds, about 35 spoken words at most. Both hosts are on screen.
3. **Both hosts, one useful visual.** A source passage, screenshot or ready result is on screen with the hosts.
4. **First useful point by about 45 seconds.** The three questions that guide the episode can come here.
5. **Housekeeping later.** Stream and audio checks happen before going live. Greetings, "how was your week" and catch-up go to an after-show block after the verdicts. A chat greeting may come after the first useful point.

## Why this rule exists

From the 10 November 2026 audit of the channel: the archives start after the countdown with "and we are live", a tech check and small talk. 1 of 35 livestreams states the topic and why it matters inside 30 seconds. On the 8 archives with retention data, a median 60% of viewers leave in the half minute after the countdown; 96% are still there when the countdown ends. This is a small sample and one auditor's reading of the openings, so treat the numbers as direction.

Edited videos that open on a claim or a firsthand moment hold best. Two openings to learn from, both from the channel's own best-rated videos:

- **Pull the strongest firsthand moment forward.** *Your SaaS Will Get Hacked* opens on the story and the number ("we did that for 400 websites within 24 hours") before any setup.
- **No ask before the first payoff.** *Claude Code Channels Killed OpenClaw* opens on a sharp claim, then asks for a like and subscribe at about 33 seconds; it loses 58% of viewers in that stretch. The claim worked; the ask cost viewers.

Question openings are fine once. Six of 34 videos open on the same shape ("Are you still using X?"). Do not make it the default.

## Build the first sentence

Write the claim first, then the stake. Use only a receipt you can show: a quote, number, incident, release or result from the board, or something a host did. Never invent a number, date, quote or result. If a fact is unchecked, mark it `CHECK BEFORE SAYING` or leave it out.

Patterns, pick the one the evidence supports:

| Pattern | Use when | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim first | The hosts hold a view | "[Claim]. If you [viewer situation], that changes [cost, risk or workflow]." |
| Receipt first | A hard number, outage or quote exists | "[Receipt]. That is not a small detail: it means [interpretation]." |
| Story first | A host has a firsthand result | "[What happened to us, with one number]. Here is what we would do about it." |
| Fight the lazy take | The audience likely has a simple take | "The lazy take is [X]. The useful take is [Y]." |

Return two or three options, each with the receipt it rests on and what to have on screen. The hosts choose one and put it in their own words. After it, nobody reads anything.

## Cut from the opening

- "We are live", sound and mic checks, "are we live", chat greetings, retweet requests
- "Welcome back", "how was your week", "today we're going to talk about…" when a stronger claim exists
- A subscribe or like ask before the first payoff
- Comments about not having an intro ("okay, intro done")
- A line that needs context the viewer does not have ("and if I say to Opus…")
- Rhetorical questions with no payoff, and generic hype words

## Check

- Does it stand alone for a viewer who joins at the replay?
- Is the topic named in the first sentence?
- One receipt, one stake, nothing invented?
- Under 15 seconds when read aloud once?
- Does it match what the segments actually deliver? A provocative opening must still describe the recording.
