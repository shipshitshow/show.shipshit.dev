## Packaging

Livestreams cover a viewer decision across sources and firsthand stories. Recaps package the actual final edit, not the entire source livestream. Shorts contain a complete, narrow point and are published on the main channel, each linked to its full episode (description link and Related video; see the metadata skill). Never borrow another creator's transcript as channel history.

**Titles (owner, 6 November 2026): one line on the home feed.** The title must fit one line on the YouTube home feed. Aim for 40 characters or fewer, never more than 45 (owner's choice; YouTube's 100-character cap and the ~60 characters visible in search are not the target). A full-width desktop feed holds about 50 characters, so 40 leaves room for smaller windows; narrow cards can cut at about 32, so put the take in the first words. Wide letters (W, M, capitals) cost more: preview the real width in the titles lab before choosing. Lead with the take or the stakes, not the product name. Spell-check names and words before upload: a live title once read "OpenAI Will Bo Bankrupt Before 2030".

Recent source examples: September 29 stream https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSuqoiEaB1k and its edited video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WOTzkAWvb0w; September 8 stream https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KPAyE6KSrQU. Inspect actual transcript and current titles before borrowing a pattern. These examples do not establish performance winners.

Descriptions speak as the show in first-person plural: **we, us, our; “Join us.”** Host names are internal context, not an introduction in public description copy. Keep the opener human and the practical takeaway specific; use verified chapters only when a recording exists.

Livestream descriptions stay concise and contain **no source list** unless explicitly requested. Keep citations and source links in the episode’s talking-points/source board. Include one public `https://show.shipshit.dev/talking-points/<YYYY-MM-DD>` link only after checking, signed out, that the rendered title and visible notes/resources match the exact episode. An HTTP 200 is insufficient: missing dates can fall back to older episodes. If the page is missing, private, empty or shows another episode, leave its URL out of the paste-ready description and report that it is pending outside the copy. Preparing metadata does not authorize publishing notes, changing topic visibility or merging/deploying code.

Recap/edited-video descriptions may include relevant source/example links when useful. Return the final title and description in separate plain-text copy blocks. Use `youtube_tags` for vault keyword lists. Suggested footer when applicable:

Subscribe so you don't miss the next build.
Follow us: https://x.com/shipshitdev

## Visual direction

Canonical source: [the vault brand kit](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/tree/master/brand), including [identity](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/identity.md), [application rules](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/applications.md), [editing rules](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/editing.md), [tokens](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/tokens.json) and [asset inventory](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/inventory.json). Read the relevant guide and actual asset status before creating show artwork. This summary is not an independent palette or asset source; when the kit changes, update this canonical profile and run the existing profile sync.

Compact mark: the standalone **six-arm asterisk**, three identical rectangular bars at 0°/60°/120° in the approved 96×96 viewport. Full name: **Ship Sh*t Show**. Baseline: **Think AI**, without colon or trailing punctuation. Use the supplied vector/component; never a font-glyph substitute, compressed lettering or a square around the thumbnail signature.

Both websites start in dark mode. Shared foundations are charcoal #0D0D0D and white #FFFFFF, with broad compact typography. Show type is Space Grotesk; the commercial site retains approved Inter Tight headings, Inter body text and JetBrains Mono code. The show uses red #E83B3B for its primary identity/actions; the commercial shipshit.dev identity is monochrome. Black text belongs on red show actions; charcoal text belongs on white business actions. Official third-party logos and semantic status/chart colors retain their actual meaning.

Main-channel avatar: red asterisk on charcoal. Commercial shipshit.dev avatar: white asterisk on charcoal. Clips: white asterisk on charcoal. Personal PFPs: consistent three-quarter framing and natural color on charcoal, without a mark on the person or backdrop. Keep host identities/proportions and eye-level gaze accurate: Vincent left, Mitchell right; use the approved original portraits/character references, never a degraded rejected render. Full heads and shoulders remain visible in website/banner masters. Use native image proportions and background-matched edge integration.

Thumbnail art (owner, locked 6 November 2026 from the approved Thumbnail Lab winners):
- **Style:** ultra-realistic photoreal. It scales well, for example a famous person flexing for a model release. Pop-art, Soviet poster, clay, painted and paper-cut styles were rejected.
- **No text** on the thumbnail: the image plus the signature only. The one exception is a single prop word the owner asks for, printed on an object in the scene (a FREE price tag, a FIRED slip), or a logo's own lettering.
- **Faces:** never Vincent's or Mitchell's. Famous people or AI influencers appear as the embodiment of their company or model, and the likeness must be convincing (an average look-alike fails). CEOs embody their companies and compete: Altman for OpenAI, Amodei for Anthropic, Musk for xAI/Grok, Zuckerberg for Meta. Alternatively, show the company's AI assistant as a character. Liked: a three-way CEO standoff, Musk with a Grok butler robot, OpenAI vs Anthropic as chess pieces. Rejected: Altman vs Amodei boxing, Amodei cutting a Codex cable.
- **Logos:** a company or model topic must show that company's official logo (Grok for Grok, Anthropic for Anthropic), large and recognisable. Pass the real logo file as a reference to copy exactly, or composite it; never invent it. Logos may sit in the scene as physical objects. Use only the logos that belong to the story (a Codex build gets Altman, not Amodei), and when a person carries the topic, the person alone is enough.
- **Readable at a glance:** one simple hero object or a big face with clear emotion (panic, a stop gesture at the lens, looking at the viewer, a face-off). Show the product or the idea literally. The edited video and the livestream on the same topic get different designs.
- **Single-model release:** only the official logo and a variation of it, nothing else.
- **"Software is dead" topics:** Amodei as the lead figure, Altman behind him.
- **Security and attack topics:** a photoreal real-life metaphor, such as a chained, padlocked shop shutter with a Bitcoin ransom, a hooded intruder backlit with a red glow, or a hacker facing a CTO. It must read at small size. No stock hacker imagery and no niche tech names (for example "PHP").
- **Shorts covers:** echo the parent video's thumbnail style, in 9:16 with the Short video mark.
- **Checks:** one main character, no clutter. The top half alone shows the subject (the 50% test). A viewer can guess the title from the image, and it works without the title. Start from what the viewer should wonder and feel.
- **Tests:** change one variable at a time (thumbnail first, then title), with variants clearly different at a glance.

Earlier rules (topic-first with no faces, optional large text; 26 October 2026) are superseded. Operator/business-use and head-to-head topics have drawn the most views on the channel and pure model news the fewest; this is from view counts alone, without CTR or retention, so it is a hypothesis. Hosts stay in website, banner, avatar and cover work. The exact thumbnail signature is always **top-right**, inside the [format-specific safe margins](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/thumbnail-safe-zones.md). For 1280×720 use x1120/y64 and a 96×96 viewport: equal 64px top/right margins. For a 1080×1920 Short video use the red master at x880/y230 in a 120×120 viewport (centre x940/y290): 230px top, 80px right. A Short cover is cropped from the top by the Shorts grid, so its mark sits lower: x880/y345 (centre x940/y405), 345px top, 80px right, same size and colour. Scale the entire template uniformly. These are house margins; the conservative vertical video-ad safe-area reference is not an official organic-thumbnail guarantee. Check actual crops and player UI. Edited-video mark: white on dark, black on light. Livestream mark: red. Use the actual [red SVG master](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/assets/asterisk-red.svg) or [white SVG master](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/assets/asterisk-white.svg) as the final editable layer. Reserve its area during generation; subtle scene-matched lighting/material is allowed while exact upright silhouette, base color, safe margins and small-size contrast stay intact. No dot, waves, box or duplicate over a baked-in signature. Never substitute static SVG portraits/layouts for requested imagegen artwork.

Asterisk placement has one lookup: [the placement table](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/placement.md), generated from the same tokens. Same three SVG masters everywhere, always top-right, uniformly scaled: 16:9 is 7.5% of the frame width (96px at x1120/y64 on 1280×720, 144px at x1680/y96 on 1920×1080); 9:16 Short covers and Short videos share one mark (the red master, 120px, 80px from the right) at two heights: centred at x940/y290 on the video (owner decision, 5 November 2026) and at x940/y405 on the cover (owner decision, 7 November 2026). Colour otherwise follows the video the image belongs to: white (black on light) for any edited video or recap thumbnail, red for an actual livestream. The owner-locked 9:16 Short video template (speaker fills the frame, no title, nothing below y≈1570) is in the table. Over a screen share the product UI wins: no persistent corner watermark, and never move the mark.

Default website social cards are 1200×630. The selected show image is host-led “Think AI” with a red bottom-right asterisk and no top-left show wordmark. The approved commercial card is monochrome “Your idea. We ship it.” Keep both Open Graph and Twitter metadata aligned with versioned exports; preserve author/episode-specific cards.

Use the actual episode/transcript for claims and final timeline for captions. Thumbnails carry no text or episode numbers and never the hosts' faces; surgical corrections preserve unrequested details. Broadcast graphics use the same geometry/type/palette with compact names/topic labels and optional narrow approved company-logo/URL strips. Test actual camera/caption/UI alignment rather than copying a concept board.

The public kit includes current standalone vector marks and clearly archived exploration. Original host photographs, character sheets, face-containing concept boards and editing projects remain private. Only separately approved finished deliverables may be published to their authorized destinations. A generated preview is not real footage or a claim that a channel upload happened. Record kit revision, actual fonts, logo hash and approval/check receipts. Collaborators obtain authorized reference media without assuming machine-local Desktop paths exist.

Channel branding and broadcast assets should feel related without reproducing the same busy composition in every scene. A PFP needs recognition at a tiny circular crop; a banner needs responsive safe-area composition; a screen demo needs room for readable product content.
