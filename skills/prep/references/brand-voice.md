# Voice

How the two hosts sound at their best, so a first sentence or a cue reads like them. Applies on top of the [channel profile](channel-profile.md); the profile owns identity, titles and brand. Evidence below comes from the 10 November 2026 audit of the repo's transcripts, so treat it as direction.

## What the voice is

- Two founders comparing notes on what AI actually does in their businesses. Direct, specific, no corporate speak.
- "I" for a host's own claim and result, "we" for shared show actions. Opinion is phrased as opinion.
- Blunt and useful: say the impolite but useful thing, with the number or the story behind it.
- One strong swear when it earns its place, never as seasoning.
- Honest about what neither of us has tried. "We did not test that" beats a confident guess.

Lines that sound like the show: "The tools are free, the harness is the IP." "Cheaper per task is not the same as cheaper per token." "Every pre-AI codebase is undiscovered bug reports."

## What the best openings do

Taken from the channel's seven best-rated videos (*Claude Code Channels Killed OpenClaw*, *pstack*, *Your SaaS Will Get Hacked*, *GPT 5.4 vs Claude Opus 4.6*, *Grok Bot Finds Customers While I Sleep*, *Moltbook*, *Composer 2.5*):

- Lead with a claim, a stake or a firsthand moment with a number, in the first seconds.
- Name the tool and the outcome. The best titles do, and the opening matches the title.
- Sound like a conversation between the two hosts, not an announcement to a crowd.
- Keep the ask for likes and subscribes until after the first payoff. *Claude Code Channels Killed OpenClaw* asks at about 33 seconds and loses 58% of its viewers in that stretch.

Do not copy their wording. Reuse the structure: claim or story, receipt, stake.

## Tics to drop on air

"Whatever", "stuff", "blah blah blah" and "I don't know" recur in every livestream and read as not caring. Replace them with a pause or a specific statement ("I did not test that"). Also drop the back-channel pile-up: when a host says "yeah", "exactly" or "mhm" instead of reacting, the viewer hears one voice. Ask what it cost, what broke, or whether they would ship it.

The median livestream runs at 147 words a minute with about 25 "uh" and "um" per 1,000 words, against 181 and about 1 in the edited videos. Speaking faster is not the goal. Fewer empty moments is.

## Rules when writing for the hosts

- Cues, not scripts. The one prepared line is the first sentence. See [the opening guide](opening-and-hooks.md).
- Never write a host's opinion, result or number for them. Mark it for the host to supply.
- No invented quotes, dates, numbers or launch details. Mark unchecked facts `CHECK BEFORE SAYING`.
- No manufactured panic, scripted catchphrases or unsupported certainty.
