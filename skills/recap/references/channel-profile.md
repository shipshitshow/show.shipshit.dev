## Packaging

Livestreams cover a viewer decision across sources and firsthand stories. Recaps package the actual final edit, not the entire source livestream. Shorts contain a complete, narrow point. Never borrow another creator's transcript as channel history.

Recent source examples: September 29 stream https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSuqoiEaB1k and its edited video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WOTzkAWvb0w; September 8 stream https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KPAyE6KSrQU. Inspect actual transcript and current titles before borrowing a pattern. These examples do not establish performance winners.

Descriptions speak as the show in first-person plural: **we, us, our; “Join us.”** Host names are internal context, not an introduction in public description copy. Keep the opener human and the practical takeaway specific; use verified chapters only when a recording exists.

Livestream descriptions stay concise and contain **no source list** unless explicitly requested. Keep citations and source links in the episode’s talking-points/source board. Include one public `https://show.shipshit.dev/talking-points/<YYYY-MM-DD>` link only after checking, signed out, that the rendered title and visible notes/resources match the exact episode. An HTTP 200 is insufficient: missing dates can fall back to older episodes. If the page is missing, private, empty or shows another episode, leave its URL out of the paste-ready description and report that it is pending outside the copy. Preparing metadata does not authorize publishing notes, changing topic visibility or merging/deploying code.

Recap/edited-video descriptions may include relevant source/example links when useful. Return the final title and description in separate plain-text copy blocks. Use `youtube_tags` for vault keyword lists. Suggested footer when applicable:

Subscribe so you don't miss the next build.
Follow us: https://x.com/shipshitdev

## Visual direction

Canonical source: [the vault brand kit](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/tree/master/brand), including [identity](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/identity.md), [application rules](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/applications.md), [editing rules](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/editing.md), [tokens](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/tokens.json) and [asset inventory](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/inventory.json). Read the relevant guide and actual asset status before creating show artwork. This summary is not an independent palette or asset source; when the kit changes, update this canonical profile and run the existing profile sync.

Compact mark: the standalone **six-arm asterisk**, three identical rectangular bars at 0°/60°/120° in the approved 96×96 viewport. Full name: **Ship Sh*t Show**. Baseline: **Think AI**, without colon or trailing punctuation. Use the supplied vector/component; never a font-glyph substitute, compressed lettering or a square around the thumbnail signature.

Both websites start in dark mode. Shared foundations are charcoal #0D0D0D and white #FFFFFF, with broad compact typography. Show type is Space Grotesk; the commercial site retains approved Inter Tight headings, Inter body text and JetBrains Mono code. The show uses red #E83B3B for its primary identity/actions; the commercial shipshit.dev identity is monochrome. Black text belongs on red show actions; charcoal text belongs on white business actions. Official third-party logos and semantic status/chart colors retain their actual meaning.

Main-channel avatar: red asterisk on charcoal. Commercial shipshit.dev avatar: white asterisk on charcoal. Clips: white asterisk on charcoal. Personal PFPs: consistent three-quarter framing and natural color on charcoal, without a mark on the person or backdrop. Keep host identities/proportions and eye-level gaze accurate: Vincent left, Mitchell right; use the approved original portraits/character references, never a degraded rejected render. Full heads and shoulders remain visible in website/banner masters. Use native image proportions and background-matched edge integration.

Thumbnails are topic-first and show no host faces (the hosts are not yet recognized; decided 26 October 2026). The subject is the episode topic: a source-grounded visual or official topic logo, which keeps its original colors, on a predominantly monochrome backdrop. Text is optional and episode-specific; large brief text may name the business use or the head-to-head. Operator/business-use and head-to-head topics have drawn the most views on the channel and pure model news the fewest; this is from view counts alone, without CTR or retention, so it is a hypothesis. Hosts stay in website, banner, avatar and cover work. The exact thumbnail signature is always **top-right**, inside the [format-specific safe margins](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/thumbnail-safe-zones.md). For 1280×720 use x1120/y64 and a 96×96 viewport: equal 64px top/right margins. For 1080×1920 Shorts use x768/y320 and a 96×96 viewport: 320px top, 216px right. Scale the entire template uniformly. These are house margins; the conservative vertical video-ad safe-area reference is not an official organic-thumbnail guarantee. Check actual crops and player UI. Edited-video mark: white on dark, black on light. Livestream mark: red. Use the actual [red SVG master](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/assets/asterisk-red.svg) or [white SVG master](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/assets/asterisk-white.svg) as the final editable layer. Reserve its area during generation; subtle scene-matched lighting/material is allowed while exact upright silhouette, base color, safe margins and small-size contrast stay intact. No dot, waves, box or duplicate over a baked-in signature. Never substitute static SVG portraits/layouts for requested imagegen artwork.

Asterisk placement has one lookup: [the placement table](https://github.com/shipshitshow/vault/blob/master/brand/placement.md), generated from the same tokens. Same three SVG masters everywhere, always top-right, uniformly scaled: 16:9 is 7.5% of the frame width (96px at x1120/y64 on 1280×720, 144px at x1680/y96 on 1920×1080); 9:16 is 8.9% (96px at x768/y320 on 1080×1920). Colour follows the video the image belongs to: white (black on light) for any edited video, recap or Short, red only for an actual livestream. Over a screen share the product UI wins: no persistent corner watermark, and never move the mark. The 9:16 Short video layout (title above, full sharp 16:9 frame centred, captions on a dark backing, bottom ~350px clear) is in the same table and awaits the owner's OK on one example.

Default website social cards are 1200×630. The selected show image is host-led “Think AI” with a red bottom-right asterisk and no top-left show wordmark. The approved commercial card is monochrome “Your idea. We ship it.” Keep both Open Graph and Twitter metadata aligned with versioned exports; preserve author/episode-specific cards.

Use the actual episode/transcript for claims and final timeline for captions. Text and episode numbers remain creative choices (faces do not appear in thumbnails); surgical corrections preserve unrequested details. Broadcast graphics use the same geometry/type/palette with compact names/topic labels and optional narrow approved company-logo/URL strips. Test actual camera/caption/UI alignment rather than copying a concept board.

The public kit includes current standalone vector marks and clearly archived exploration. Original host photographs, character sheets, face-containing concept boards and editing projects remain private. Only separately approved finished deliverables may be published to their authorized destinations. A generated preview is not real footage or a claim that a channel upload happened. Record kit revision, actual fonts, logo hash and approval/check receipts. Collaborators obtain authorized reference media without assuming machine-local Desktop paths exist.

Channel branding and broadcast assets should feel related without reproducing the same busy composition in every scene. A PFP needs recognition at a tiny circular crop; a banner needs responsive safe-area composition; a screen demo needs room for readable product content.
